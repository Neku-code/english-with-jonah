PRESENTATION CHANGES

Reference: the supplied Side by Side PDF and the four supplied image examples.

The useful pattern in the reference is a visible situation beside a short model. Pictures supply the people, objects, setting, or state needed to understand the language. A small set of model sentences then supports several variations. Reading and listening bring the same language into another context.

The previous drafts relied heavily on rule blocks and repeated am/is/are selection. The revision starts with a drawn situation, presents a small model, gives a short Uzbek explanation, and moves through picture cues, sentence construction, reading, listening, and independent use.

Images now carry information: who is speaking; whether the speaker belongs to a group; where a person is; what a place's open/closed sign means; which statement needs correcting; and how a question relates to its answer. Speech bubbles attach the English to the correct speaker. They are model examples, so later text and listening tasks supply independent checks.

The American university settings and varied adult characters follow the user's correction. Familiar Uzbek names and places remain valid in personal answers. Jonah is the app's name, not a character required in every picture.

The reference's classroom pair work has been replaced by tasks that work alone. Texts, scenes, names, question order, and tasks are original. The book's chapter sequence and layouts have not been reproduced.

Answers, vocabulary, audio scripts, and images are separate from the student lesson. The final test has its own picture without model-answer bubbles, and it introduces no new assessed language.
